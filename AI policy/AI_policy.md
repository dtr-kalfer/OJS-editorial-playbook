# Journal Policy on the Use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in Publishing
*(Formulated in accordance with DOAJ Transparency Criteria, COPE Position Statements & Flowcharts, ICMJE Recommendations, and WAME Guidance on Generative AI)*

---

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This policy governs the responsible, transparent, and ethical use of **Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI)** and **AI-assisted technologies** (e.g., large language models such as ChatGPT/GPT-4, Gemini, Claude, Copilot; image generators such as DALL·E, Midjourney; and AI-based analytical or translation tools) across all stages of the scholarly publishing workflow.

It applies equally to:
- **Authors** (manuscript preparation, data analysis, figure creation);
- **Peer Reviewers** (evaluation of submitted manuscripts);
- **Editors and Editorial Staff** (editorial decision-making and correspondence).

This policy is maintained in line with the **Principles of Transparency and Best Practice in Scholarly Publishing** (DOAJ, COPE, OASPA, WAME) and will be reviewed annually as AI technologies and community standards evolve.

## 3. Policy for Authors

### 3.1. AI Cannot Be an Author
* Consistent with the **COPE Position Statement**, AI tools **cannot be listed as an author**, nor meet ICMJE authorship criteria.
* Authorship requires the capacity to take public responsibility for the work, approve the final version, and be accountable for its integrity — capabilities that AI systems do not possess.
* Any AI tool used shall be declared in the *Acknowledgments* or *Methods*, never in the author list.

### 3.2. Permissible Uses of AI (With Mandatory Disclosure)
Authors **may** use AI tools, provided all use is transparently disclosed, for:
- Improving grammar, spelling, readability, and linguistic polish;
- Restructuring text written by the authors themselves;
- Brainstorming or refining presentation of ideas already conceived by the authors;
- Assisting with code development or debugging (code must still be verified and tested by the authors);
- Summarizing the authors' own drafts for internal revision purposes.

### 3.3. Prohibited Uses of AI
Authors **must not** use AI tools to:
- Generate, fabricate, or falsify data, datasets, or experimental results;
- Create or digitally alter images, micrographs, gels, or figures presented as empirical evidence (unless AI is itself the object/method of study — see §6);
- Generate citations, references, or quotations without rigorous verification (AI-fabricated "hallucinated" references constitute misconduct);
- Write substantive scientific content (rationale, interpretation, conclusions) that the authors cannot personally defend, verify, and claim as their own intellectual work;
- Reproduce copyrighted material or plagiarize existing publications via paraphrasing engines.

### 3.4. Mandatory Disclosure Format
Manuscripts using any AI-assisted technology must include a declaration in the **Declarations section** (Title Page), e.g.:

> *"During the preparation of this work, the authors used [Tool Name, Version/Developer] to improve the readability and language of the manuscript. After using this tool/service, the authors reviewed and edited the content as needed and take full responsibility for the integrity and accuracy of the published article."*

If no AI was used, the following statement is recommended:
> *"The authors declare that no generative AI tools were used in the preparation of this manuscript."*

## 4. Policy for Peer Reviewers

### 4.1. Confidentiality Comes First
* Submitted manuscripts are **confidential documents**. Reviewers **must not upload, paste, or otherwise transmit manuscripts, supplementary materials, or images into generative AI tools**, as doing so may expose unpublished research to third parties and violate author confidentiality and data protection obligations.
* This prohibition extends to AI features embedded in productivity software (e.g., AI assistants in email clients, browsers, or word processors) where content may be transmitted externally.

### 4.2. AI Cannot Perform Peer Review
* Reviewers must not delegate the critical assessment, judgment, opinion, or recommendation components of a review to AI.
* AI may only be used sparingly for a reviewer's own language polishing of their review comments, never to generate the substance of the evaluation.
* Any suspected breach will be handled under COPE flowcharts and may result in removal from the reviewer pool.

---

## 5. Policy for Editors and Editorial Staff

* **Editorial decisions must never be delegated to AI.** Desk decisions, reviewer selection, acceptance/rejection judgments, and ethics determinations remain exclusively human responsibilities.
* Editors must not transmit unpublished manuscripts or author correspondence into external AI tools.
* Editors are trained to recognize undisclosed AI-related risks (fabricated references, synthetic imagery, incoherent methodology) and will act under COPE guidance when concerns arise.
* Administrative uses of AI (e.g., scheduling, formatting reminders, anonymization support) are permitted where no confidential content leaves controlled systems.

---

## 6. AI as a Research Subject (Exception)

Where **AI is itself the subject of the research** (e.g., studies evaluating model performance, AI-assisted diagnostics, LLM benchmarking):
- Use of AI outputs is legitimate scientific inquiry and must be documented methodologically;
- The Methods section must specify model names, versions, prompts, parameters, and validation procedures sufficient for replication;
- Limitations, biases, and reproducibility constraints of the models must be transparently discussed.

---

## 7. AI Detection Screening

* The journal acknowledges that current **AI-detection tools produce unreliable and biased results** (documented false positives, particularly among non-native English speakers).
* Accordingly, AI-detector scores alone will **never** serve as the sole basis for rejection or misconduct allegations.
* Concerns triggered by detection tools will prompt a human-led investigation consistent with **COPE flowcharts**, focusing on evidence (e.g., unverifiable references, fabricated data) rather than scores.

---

## 8. Non-Compliance and Enforcement

| Violation | Consequence |
| :--- | :--- |
| Undisclosed minor AI assistance (e.g., language editing) | Request for retroactive disclosure; editorial warning |
| AI-generated citations/references that cannot be verified | Revision demanded; possible rejection; institutional notification if pattern persists |
| AI-fabricated data, results, or images | Immediate rejection/retraction per COPE Retraction Guidelines; author's institution notified; submission ban considered |
| Reviewer uploading manuscripts to AI tools | Removal from reviewer database; notification of editorial board |

All investigations follow the journal's Publication Ethics & Malpractice Policy (`cope_rules.md`) and applicable COPE flowcharts.
